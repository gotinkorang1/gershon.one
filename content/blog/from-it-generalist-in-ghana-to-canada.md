I used to be the network admin, the help desk, the web developer, and the server guy, all in one role. That’s normal where I’m from.

In Ghana, many IT professionals become generalists out of necessity: in a single role you might support users, manage the network, maintain the website, fix hardware, run servers, and help deliver digital projects, often all in the same week. You learn to solve whatever lands on your desk.

In Newfoundland, and across Canada more broadly, roles tend to be more specialized. Employers often look for clearly defined responsibilities, formal processes, strong documentation, recognized certifications, and Canadian work experience.

For newcomers, that shift can be humbling. You may arrive with years of hands-on experience and still find yourself learning the local market, rebuilding your network from scratch, and figuring out how to describe what you’ve done in a way Canadian employers recognize.

But here’s what I keep reminding myself:

We are not starting from zero. We are learning to translate.

The skills are real. The problem-solving is real. The adaptability that comes from wearing many hats is an asset.

The work now is learning the language of a new environment, and that takes time, patience, and community.

To fellow internationally trained professionals: what helped you most in making that translation?

And to Canadian hiring managers: what do you wish newcomers knew about presenting their experience?
